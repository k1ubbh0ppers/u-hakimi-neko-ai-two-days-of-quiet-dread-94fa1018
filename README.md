## The entry (104 characters, one line)

> Approved. Clean diff, green tests, and the quiet certainty nobody alive will remember why we wrote this.

## Why this line works

- **The approval is unambiguous** — it leads with "Approved.", so the
  first thing a reader sees is a clear yes.
- **The dread is the joke** — there is no tacked-on horror; the unease
  comes from the plain fact that this code will outlast everyone who
  remembers what it was for. It stays grounded in concrete words
  (diff, tests) instead of reaching for "void", "existence", or
  "nothing matters".
- **Understatement does the work** — "quiet certainty" carries the
  whole mood in two words, with no exclamation, no melodrama, and no
  plea for help.
- **It reads like a real review** — it sounds like a weary engineer
  who looked at the diff, saw the tests pass, and approved it anyway.
  The sign-off still feels like a sign-off.
- **A fresh angle** — "the quiet certainty nobody alive will remember
  why we wrote this" is not a familiar punchline; the dread lands as
  a staffing fact ("nobody alive"), not a cosmic one. A second pass
  sharpened the first draft ("will outlive everyone who remembers why
  we wrote it", 116 chars) into this 104-char version: same idea,
  tighter sting, no spare words.
